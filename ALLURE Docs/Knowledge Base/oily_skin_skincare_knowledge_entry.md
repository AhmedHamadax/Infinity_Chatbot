# **Oily Skin - Skincare Recommendation Knowledge Entry** 

## **Skin type** 

- Oily skin, including oily skin that is acne-prone or prone to breakouts and shine. 

## **Recommended product categories** 

- Exfoliating cleanser, including cleansers or washes with salicylic acid. 

- Exfoliating toner containing salicylic acid, glycolic acid, or lactic acid. 

- Acne/oil-control treatments such as benzoyl peroxide or sulfur for daytime use. 

- Retinol product for evening use. 

- Lightweight moisturizer for morning and evening. 

- Sunscreen with at least SPF 30.

- Blotting papers for excess oil during the day. 

- OTC products with effective active ingredients; dermatologist-directed treatments if OTC products do not help. 

## **Preferred product characteristics** 

- Moisturizer: lightweight, oil-free, noncomedogenic, and water-based. 

- Products formulated for acne-prone skin are presented as fitting the moisturizer criteria above. 

- Sunscreen: at least SPF 30; the article also mentions sunscreens containing titanium dioxide or zinc oxide. 

- The article notes that the term "natural" is not regulated across the industry. 

## **Products or practices to avoid** 

- Avoid products containing alcohol, which the article says can increase oil secretion. 

- Avoid thick or greasy products such as cocoa butter, shea butter, and Vaseline. 

- Do not skip cleansing after exercise or skip sunscreen. 

- Benzoyl peroxide and salicylic acid can cause irritation; use them as instructed. 

- Do not take oral isotretinoin without a dermatologist's advice. 

## **Important considerations** 

- Suggested daily routine: cleanse, tone, treat, and moisturize in both the morning and evening. 

- Treatment depends on the specific concern. For acne-prone skin, the article suggests benzoyl peroxide or sulfur in the daytime and retinol in the evening. 

- Cleansing should remove dirt, sebum, and bacteria without stripping moisture. 

- Benzoyl peroxide can be drying and irritating; start slowly and check for irritation. 

- After exercise, cleanse as soon as practical and apply a light layer of moisturizer. 

- Blotting papers can be gently pressed on skin as needed during the day to absorb excess oil. 

- If OTC products do not help, the article recommends seeing a dermatologist; oral isotretinoin is described as a dermatologist-directed option. 

- Home-remedy ingredients mentioned include honey, oatmeal, and jojoba; they are presented as possible options, not core routine requirements.
